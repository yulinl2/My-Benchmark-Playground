Problem 3
Assume you have to cut a very rare diamond. Before doing so, you want to ensure that the
settings of your cutting machine are ﬁnely tuned to minimize the cutting force applied to the
diamond. This can reduce power requirements, elastic distortion of the diamond, and tool wear.
To do so, we ﬁrst conduct a series of experiments. The ﬁrst variable considered is the type of
cutting tool, denoted by chamfer or hone, in the DIAMOND dataset. If chamfer is chosen, there
are two additional variables, angle and length, required to be speciﬁed. The remaining variables
are edge radius, rake angle, nose radius, cutting speed, feed, and depth of cut. The last column is
the response variable, the cutting force.
(1). First, ignore the columns “angle” and “length”. Based on the remaining seven variables,
ﬁt a Gaussian process model. Please describe your assumptions, the ﬁtted model, and the
estimated parameters. Then, calculate the mean squared prediction error based on the ﬁtted
model.
(2). Based on this cutting process, which of the seven variables has the greatest eﬀect on the
cutting force? Please provide detailed reasoning.
(3). Plot the predicted cutting force along with its pointwise 95% percent prediction conﬁdence
intervals as a function of the variable you identiﬁed in (2).
(4). Based on model (1), ﬁnd the optimal setting that minimizes the cutting force. Under this
optimal setting, what is the predicted cutting force and its prediction uncertainty?
(5). If we need to incorporate both “angle” and “length” and ﬁt a single model based on these
nine variables, what would be a reasonable choice of model? Please describe your model and
the assumptions (It does not have to be a Gaussian process model, and you do not need to
analyze the data.).
