Problem 1
Background
Alzheimer’s disease (AD) neuropathology, including beta-amyloid plaques, neurodegenration
and neuroﬁbrillary tangles, begins to develop at least 10-20 years before a clinical diagnosis of
dementia is given. One hypothesis regarding the reason for failed clinical trials is that the treatment
is initiated too late in the disease, after neuronal death has occurred. With novel technologies, it
may now be possible to identify markers (predictors) of AD during a preclinical disease phase
prior to symptom onset. For example, cerebrospinal ﬂuid (CSF) acquired from the lumbar section
of the spinal cord can be assayed for proteins associated with beta-amyloid plaques (ab 1 42),
neurodegeneration (total tau [ttau]) and neuroﬁbrillary tangles (phosphorylated tau [ptau]), all of
which are in turn are associated with AD.
In individuals with AD, the typical CSF proﬁle is low ab 1 42 (reﬂecting amyloid protein ac-
cumulated in plaques in the brain rather than releasing through the CSF) and high total and
phosphorylated tau (reﬂecting breakdown of neurons due to neuroﬁbrillary tangles). An important
goal of current studies is to identify AD accurately and reliably in the earliest stages of disease.
This enables future clinical trials to enroll participants at highest risk for developing AD to test
if early interventions slow or prevent progression of cognitive decline, leading to AD. To do that,
it is ﬁrst necessary to know if biomarkers of disease in preclinical AD accurately predict cognitive
decline.
Methods
Data for the current study are drawn from a dataset collected between 2001 and 2016 in a
community sample of about 1500 late middle-aged adults (mean age at baseline = 54). The co-
horts were designed to be enriched for higher AD risk (e.g., ˜75% with a family history of AD).
Participants return for study visits every 2 years (with the exceptions of 4 years between visits 1
and 2 for a subset and 1 year between visits for older participants/participants with AD or mild
cognitive impairment (MCI)) and undergo neuropsychological cognitive evaluations and medical
exams. Lumbar punctures to obtain CSF were conducted on approximately 500 participants as
part of a linked study.
Cognitive status at each study visit was determined by a consensus panel based on review of
cognitive performance at all visits and review of additional information in each participant’s chart.
Possible cognitive status outcomes include: 0) cognitively normal, 1) early MCI, 2) clinical MCI,
3) dementia, or 4) impairment-not MCI. CSF samples were assayed for several proteins, including
ab 1 42, ttau, and ptau. With the exception of Category 4), these outcomes are ordered: 0), 1),
