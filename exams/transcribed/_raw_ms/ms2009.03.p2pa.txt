          1.2    2.3   4 .3    5.4    7.6    8.4   10.2    14.5

3. Unborn babies of pregnant woman infected with HIV, are at risk at contracting HIV at birth.   A doctor 
believes when there are twins, the one who is born first has a bigger chance to be HIV infected than does 
the one who is born second.  He has data from 200 mothers infected with HIV that had twins; a) for 50 of 
these mothers both twins were born infected with HIV; b) for 20 mothers the first born twin was infected 
with HIV and the second born twin was not; c) for 10 the second born twin was infected with HIV and the 
first born was not and, d) for 120 mothers neither twin was infected.
A. Use a large sample approximation to derive the best test statistic for the null hypothesis 
that the first and second born twins are equally likely to be HIV infected and compare 
this to the rejection region at a two sided α = 0.05.
B. Based on this data if a randomly chosen twin born to an HIV infected mother is infected 
with HIV, give a point estimate and 95% confidence interval for the probability that the 
sibling twin (the chosen twin’s twin brother or sister) is also infected with HIV  Assume 
that the order of birth does not matter for this problem.

4. Two methods (Method A and Method B) to produce vials of a vaccine are tested with the outcome being 
that the vial is either good or bad. Each method is allowed to produce new vials one at a time until the first 
bad vial from that method was observed, then production using that method is stopped.  In other words,
•
For Methods A & B each, if the last vial produced is good the method is allowed to produce 
another vial. 
•
But once a bad vial is produced, the Method is stopped and no new vials are produced with that 
method.
A.  Assume that the probability for a vial produced by Method A to be bad is p = 0.05 and the probability 
for a vial produced by Method B to be bad is 0.10 and all vials are independent. 
        i) What is the expected total number of good vials produced by both Methods A and B combined 
            before both methods are stopped?
       ii) What is the variance of the total number of good vials produced by both Methods A and B 
            combined before both methods are stopped?
B.  Now assume that the probabilities for vials produced by each method to be bad are 
      unknown and the following results are observed:
•
Method A - On its first try, the vial was bad so production stopped 
•
Method B produced 15 good vials but on its 16th try, the vial was bad so production was 
stopped then
       Derive the appropriate model that conditions on 15 bad vials being produced and give a P-value for 
       observing a result this extreme if each method is equally likely to produce a bad vial.
